For me, the question of autonomy is one of sentience. 

Throughout much of my life, I have felt a dizzying sense of autopilot. Of course, I understand the wisdom a la Clear's *Atomic Habits*, but even in the midst of certain flow states or fits of anxiety—there's an odd sense of egolessness to the whole ordeal. "I'm not really there," I'll metacognitvely vex as I continue to just keep going in spite of whatever dust has settled onto my plate. I don't like it here, I suppose. My belly can be full and all of my carnal needs satisfied, but I know that I am seriously lacking in *it* on a regular basis. My Teacher told me [this was a good thing](https://noahie.xyz/blog/cogito/04-2026/storms), but I might be vexed on what appears to be a lack of repentance. For as much as I try to think through things and find a sense of progress, I still find myself greatly stalled. But on a quick second pass, I think my temporal perspective is warped here. I am too focused on the harvest, and not the nature of seasons themselves. True repentance isn't something that happens in an instant, but is one that is found gradually. Perhaps I'm just afraid of looking back.
