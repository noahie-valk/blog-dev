Why is it that everything I see is a different shade of gray?

I find my lack of discernment in many things a difficult affair, and while I try my best to retain a radical sense of honesty, I think my most pressing problem is that of intellectualizing; it's a bad defense mechanism of mine. While Cogito is part art, part neurocognitive plumbing, and part *something else entirely*, there's a difficult reality I find myself confronting when I come here to write my thoughts: I am using this writing to hide. What's interesting about the open web, especially these days in particular, is that writing on a basic website essentially functions as hiding in plain sight. For as candid as I can be, and for as open about my identity as I am, the most intimate parts of myself still fly far below the radar. It can be a sad thing to think about—maybe as a degradation of broader independent thought—but I'd rather see it as a beautiful irony; my diary is on the Internet, and it simply doesn't matter.

Today, I was supposed to take my mom to a doctor's appointment—a cortisone shot in her knee, which has been giving her much grief lately. We both forgot, in spite of my efforts to write it down and remind ourselves of it the prior night. I woke up this morning, and immediately lost focus; the few simple things I am obligated to do, I cannot. I'm at my wit's end as to how I can better manage my minimal responsibilities, and I suppose that's why I am three years into my SSDI case. It's not that I've become despondent, but that I lament my inattentiveness to the things that actually matter. Instead, I am preoccupied with darting interests and fixations, and while I can recognize fundamentally that those things are meaningless, I find myself less able to do so in practice. I guess missing today's appointment was a good enough ego-check; it won't make me lose hope in the process, though. 

I've become less enthralled with my self-examination, and I forged this degradation of pace [a few months ago](https://noahie.xyz/blog/cogito/08-2026/rest-up/) mostly because I have become more secure with my identity and position as a writer. I am not part of the literati, not through failure, but as a way to maintain my integrity and sanity. Moreover, I understand that I am still quite young, and that I am in no position to say I am anywhere close to finished. This is why I prioritize sanity above all else; the work *must* be finished as it was assigned. I have no qualms with a degree of perfectionism, and most certainly none with affairs as myopic as "legacy"—no, I understand that I'm a flash in the pan. An Aquinas quote of dubious attribution:

>Such secrets have been revealed to me that all I have written now appears as so much straw.

I can say that my mystical experiences have provided a similar reconciliation, but I don't want to be so bold as to call them revelations of any kind. I am not skeptical of their veracity, but I wish to remain humble. I understand the treasures I've been given, but I also understand that not everyone sees them as such. This makes sharing them difficult, but the blessing lies in the beauty of being unable to share them in their entirety. I understand that this act of treasure-sharing must always remain small, and always remain hidden in plain sight. My Lord likes to do the same, and I respect that deeper aspect of His teachings. 

If I tell you, you will not believe; and if I ask a question, you will not answer.
