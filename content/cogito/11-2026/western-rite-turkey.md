I just do it to feel good about myself.

I feel that all I am is a series of misguided impulses. One chase after another, so it seems. With the weather getting cooler, I've been smoking more. I even made the jump to getting RYO equipment. There's a danger in that, but at this point, I don't care. A long time ago, I learned it's not the world's fault I'm like this, but even though I try to forgive myself for my sins, I keep giving myself more reasons to fall flat. How can I protect myself and others from this tiring, all-engulfing madness? I don't want to be an agent of disorder, but I know that my capacity for wrongdoing has to be culled somehow. Sure, I could get out there, but I feel I'd be a net negative to society if I did. Not only am I unworthy of making the world better, I also don't want to.

It's tiring to try and figure out where this persistent emptiness comes from. Elusive as it may be, I still make efforts to understand it. I've learned that everything I think I know is wrong, mostly as a matter of circumstance. Maybe it stems from feeling like I don't belong anywhere, but it confounds me when I encounter kindness and affirmation from others. How can I be so loved, yet so misfitted? It makes me realize that I have a profound, agonizing separation from God. The constant awareness of this separation leaves me at times deeply despondent, but it's even worse when I feel it for a little while, and then it goes away.

Here in America, we just celebrated Thanksgiving. Words from the Blessed [Dmitri of Dallas](https://www.stseraphim.org/archbishop-dmitri-of-dallas):

>I will never bless the breaking of the fast, but know this! I will be having turkey on Thanksgiving.

An interesting perspective from my catechism: Starting at the beginning of this year, I was attending a large Orthodox cathedral, but recently switched parishes. I began attending a Western Rite parish under Antioch about a month or two ago. As I've been slowly incorporating the Orthodox lifestyle, an interesting caveat emerged. Historically, the Nativity fast begins on November 15th and runs until Christmas—forty days for obvious spiritual reasons. I read about it, and began the fast on November 15th. Turns out, my parish officially starts the fast on December 1st.

I switched to my current parish for practical reasons. It was a better drive, and I was looking for a smaller community. I also get to stay seated during services, which is more comfortable for me. However, observing a more Anglican-based rite with Orthodox theology is a beautiful synthesis of my heritage and faith. It's the first time I've truly felt at home with Christ.

I had turkey today.
