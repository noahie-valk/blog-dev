A man is a coin, or something.

Getting out of my daily writing cadence has been a fair break, and while I'd like to say I've replaced it with more fruitful things, I don't think that's entirely true. At home, we have a keyboard that's been lying around, and I moved it to my room in an effort to pick up a second instrument. It's been a fair enough pursuit, but I find myself darting between things yet again. Progress on other projects essentially halted as well, and it's mostly been because I am too fatigued to pursue them. Instead, I've just been socializing and the like. Online conversations are a low-stakes activity, but too much of it lowers my cognitive outlook. Perhaps being so tired is fine, and while my mood feels stable, I know that's mostly a product of maintenance. I find myself pursuing lower stress, and even when I do things to take care of myself, I find that I am still stuck in a perpetual state of dissatisfaction. Not only am I bereft of purpose, but also of desire.

I've resorted to writing some more recent posts on my phone. My typing skill atrophied quite a bit after ditching my iPhone some year or two ago, and switching over to a FOSS smartphone keyboard demonstrated just how much I relied on Apple's tech. Fast typing is a strange thing to be so accustomed to, but then again my mental state makes me think everything is an affront to God. I'd like to be wrong.

Even beyond a sense of ambition, there's no fruit in any pursuit. I mostly find this to emerge from a general disgust toward avarice and vanity; there's enough of that in the world. So far, my principal sin has been gluttony. The drugs help me get through the day, and that's about it. Otherwise, I have no intensive pursuit toward spiritual perfection. I've been putting off prayer memorization for *months*, and it stems from a lack of control regarding my gluttonous impulses. Fuck being "useful," and fuck a sense of anything feeling "earned." Every day is a meager attempt to appreciate grace, and I find myself at odds with savoring my gifts. Knowing I'm not worthy of receiving them only does so much—and while I *should* feel emboldened to be generous with God's bounty, I beat myself up when I choose to just survive instead. 

It's foolish to say that suffering makes for good art. Good art comes from enlightened perspective, particularly literary art. It's not an act of development, but one of perception. The truest act of art-making comes from forcing a perspective on a reader, and it's at its best when the reader isn't aware of it happening. That's why Cogito will only ever be *okay*, because there's too much meta-cognition here. But it's for me, so that I can survive and act beyond a market-driven impulse; there's nothing wrong with that.

While I wish to get off my ass and make a greater work, like finishing [Somnia](https://noahie.xyz/somnia) for instance, I know that it takes time. Inspiration can only be hacked so far. Truthfully, I have no plans for the immediate next plot acts, and I am biding my time until it comes. I know where it will end, but the messy middle seems to be the hardest part for me. Still, I'm hoping for it to be a good first book. I have no plans to make myself a Harper Lee, though. No, I'm bound to render unto Caesar what is his.
